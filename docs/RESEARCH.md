## 1. Title 5 Septic System Inspections

### What
Mandatory inspection of on-site septic systems when property transfers ownership.

### When
- **Primary**: Within **2 years before** the time of transfer
- **Alternative**: Within **6 months after** transfer (if weather conditions prevent inspection at time of sale)
- **Report Validity**: 2 years standard; extended to 3 years if system pumped annually

### Who
- **Responsible Party**: Seller
- **Inspector**: Massachusetts-certified Title 5 System Inspector
- **Enforcing Authority**: Local Board of Health, Massachusetts DEP

### Requirements
1. Seller must notify buyer in writing of the requirements of 310 CMR 15.300
2. System inspection must be performed by certified inspector
3. Inspection report submitted to local Board of Health
4. If system fails, upgrades required before or shortly after sale (6-month post-sale window if pre-approved)

### Exemptions
- **Family Transfers**: Transfers between spouses, parents and children, or full siblings
- **New/Recent Systems**: Properties with systems that received Certificate of Compliance from Board of Health within last 2 years (extended to 3 years with annual pumping records)
- **Non-residential**: Commercial-only properties

### Key Decision Tree
```
Property has septic system?
├─ YES → Built/upgraded in last 2-3 years?
│         ├─ YES → Has Certificate of Compliance? → EXEMPT
│         └─ NO → Title 5 inspection required (2 years prior OR 6 months post)
└─ NO (city sewer) → Not applicable
```

### Timeline Impact
- Schedule inspection **3-4 months before closing** (allow time for repairs if needed)
- If failed inspection: repairs can take **2-6 weeks** depending on scope
- Reminder triggers: P&S+7 days, Closing-60 days, Closing-30 days

### Citations
- **Regulation**: 310 CMR 15.000 - Septic Systems ("Title 5")
- **Authority**: Massachusetts Department of Environmental Protection (DEP)
- **Official Page**: https://www.mass.gov/guides/buying-or-selling-property-with-a-septic-system
- **Full Regulation**: https://www.mass.gov/regulations/310-CMR-15000-septic-systems-title-5

## 2. Smoke & Carbon Monoxide Detector Certificates

### What
Mandatory inspection and Certificate of Compliance for smoke detectors and carbon monoxide alarms at property sale/transfer.

### When
- **Timing**: Upon sale or transfer of residential property
- **Certificate Validity**: 60 days after issuance
- **Recommended**: Schedule as soon as Purchase & Sale Agreement is signed

### Who
- **Responsible Party**: Seller
- **Inspector**: Local Fire Department (head of fire department or designee)
- **Scheduling**: Varies by municipality (some have online portals, others require phone call)

### Requirements

#### Smoke Detectors (M.G.L. c.148 §26F)
- **Applicability**: Residential properties built or modified **before January 1, 1975** with 1-5 dwelling units
- **Requirement**: Must be equipped with approved smoke detectors upon sale/transfer
- **Placement**: Per Massachusetts State Building Code and local fire code requirements

#### Carbon Monoxide Alarms (M.G.L. c.148 §26F½)
- **Applicability**: **ALL residential dwellings** regardless of year built or number of units
- **Requirement**: Must obtain Certificate of Compliance for CO alarm compliance
- **Placement**: On every level of residence and within 10 feet of bedroom doors

### Process
1. Contact local fire department to schedule inspection (typically 2-7 days lead time)
2. Inspector visits property to verify detector placement, functionality, and compliance
3. If compliant, Fire Department issues Certificate of Compliance
4. Certificate must be provided at closing (valid 60 days)
5. If non-compliant, install/upgrade detectors and schedule re-inspection

### Fees
- Vary by municipality (typically $50-$100)
- Examples:
  - Melrose: $50 (single-family), $100 (two-family)
  - Boston: Available via online Fire Prevention Portal

### Timeline Impact
- Schedule inspection **2-3 weeks before closing**
- Allow **1 week buffer** for re-inspection if issues found
- Reminder triggers: P&S+3 days, Closing-21 days, Closing-10 days

### Citations
- **Smoke Detector Law**: M.G.L. Chapter 148, Section 26F
- **CO Alarm Law**: M.G.L. Chapter 148, Section 26F½
- **Official Guide**: https://www.mass.gov/doc/consumer-guide-to-smoke-detectors-when-selling-home/download
- **Boston Portal**: https://www.boston.gov/departments/fire-prevention/smoke-and-carbon-monoxide-detector-inspections

### Municipal Examples
- Boston Fire Prevention Portal: Online application available
- Melrose Fire Dept: Phone scheduling, 60-day certificate validity
- Wakefield Fire Dept: Standard placement requirements documented online

## 3. Lead Paint Disclosure

### What
Property Transfer Lead Paint Notification - mandatory disclosure for properties built before 1978.

### When
- **Trigger**: When prospective buyer (or tenant with option to buy) is about to purchase a home built before 1978
- **Timing**: Must be provided **before** purchase agreement is signed (Federal: 10 days for inspection period)

### Who
- **Responsible Parties**: Seller AND Real Estate Agent(s)
- **Enforcing Authorities**: Massachusetts Department of Public Health (DPH), EPA
- **Penalty Authority**: Civil penalties (MA), civil + criminal penalties (Federal)

### Requirements (105 CMR 460.720)

Seller must disclose:

1. **Compliance Certification Status**:
   - Whether premises have been certified in compliance with 105 CMR 460.750(A)(1) and 460.760(D)(2)
   - Copy of any Letter of Full Compliance or Letter of Interim Control

2. **Inspection/Assessment Records**:
   - Any lead hazard inspections performed
   - Risk assessment reports
   - Lead determination reports
   - Post-compliance assessment reports

3. **Known Information**:
   - Known presence of lead-based paint or lead hazards
   - Known records or reports on lead in the property

### Real Estate Agent Responsibilities
- Provide required information and materials to prospective purchaser
- **Verbally inform** buyer of:
  - Possible presence of dangerous levels of lead
  - Provisions of lead law and regulations
  - Purchaser's responsibility to bring property into compliance if child under 6 will reside there

### Federal Requirements
- EPA Lead-Based Paint Disclosure (for pre-1978 homes)
- Buyer must receive EPA pamphlet "Protect Your Family from Lead in Your Home"
- Buyer has right to 10-day lead inspection period (can waive)
- Disclosure form must be signed by seller, buyer, and agents

### Penalties
- **Massachusetts**: Civil penalty for non-compliance
- **Federal (EPA)**: Up to **$11,000 per error** on the disclosure form
- Both civil and criminal penalties possible under federal law

### Timeline Impact
- Provide disclosure **at offer stage** or **before P&S signing**
- If buyer requests inspection: add 10 days to timeline (unless waived)
- Reminder triggers: Pre-offer preparation, offer acceptance, P&S-5 days

### Citations
- **Massachusetts Regulation**: 105 CMR 460.000 - Lead Poisoning Prevention and Control
- **Specific Section**: 105 CMR 460.720 - Property Transfer Lead Notification and Disclosure
- **Official Page**: https://www.mass.gov/info-details/property-transfer-lead-paint-notification
- **Full Regulation**: https://www.mass.gov/doc/105-cmr-460-lead-poisoning-prevention-and-control/download
- **Federal Law**: 42 U.S.C. 4852d (Residential Lead-Based Paint Hazard Reduction Act)

## 4. Agency Disclosure

### What
Massachusetts Mandatory Licensee-Consumer Relationship Disclosure - explains agency relationships between real estate licensees and consumers.

### When (Traditional Requirement)
- **Original**: At the **first personal meeting** with a consumer to discuss a specific property

### When (Updated Requirement - 2023)
- **Current**: Must be presented **electronically or through some other means before the licensee enters into a contract** with a consumer
- **Change Rationale**: Many scenarios where licensee may never have personal meeting; definition of "personal meeting" was unclear

### Who
- **Responsible Party**: Real Estate Licensee (agent/broker)
- **Recipients**: Buyers and sellers
- **Enforcing Authority**: Massachusetts Board of Registration of Real Estate Brokers and Salespersons

### Requirements
1. Present disclosure form before entering into contract with consumer
2. Explain different types of agency relationships:
   - Seller's Agent
   - Buyer's Agent
   - Disclosed Dual Agent
   - Facilitator (limited agency)
3. Form is **not a contract** itself but must be presented before contractual relationship begins

### Exemptions
- **Not required** at open houses
- Not required for casual property discussions without specific property focus

### Timeline Impact
- Present **before listing agreement** (seller)
- Present **before buyer agency agreement** or **before showing properties** (buyer)
- Reminder triggers: Initial contact, first meeting scheduled

### Citations
- **Official Form**: Massachusetts Mandatory Licensee-Consumer Relationship Disclosure
- **Authority**: Massachusetts Board of Registration of Real Estate Brokers and Salespersons
- **Official Form**: https://www.mass.gov/doc/real-estate-board-agency-disclosure-form-english/download
- **Guidance**: https://www.mass.gov/info-details/re02r24-buyer-agency

## 5. Condominium 6(d) Certificate

### What
Certification regarding unpaid common expenses for condominium units, issued by the condominium association.

### When
- **Request Timing**: Typically **7-10 days before closing** (law requires issuance within 10 days of written request)
- **Validity**: Statement is binding once issued; must be recorded within **150 days** if being filed

### Who
- **Requesting Party**: Seller's attorney (or buyer's attorney/lender)
- **Issuing Authority**: Condominium Board of Trustees/Management Company
- **Enforcing Authority**: Massachusetts Condominium Law (M.G.L. c.183A)

### Requirements (M.G.L. c.183A §6(d))

Certificate must include:

1. **Common Expenses**: Monthly condominium fees owed
2. **Special Assessments**: Any unpaid special assessments
3. **Priority Amounts**: Statement of amounts entitled to **super-lien priority** over first mortgage (up to 6 months unpaid fees + attorney's fees)
4. **Other Charges**: Late fees, attorney's fees, fines, interest

### Types of Certificates

#### "Clean" 6(d) Certificate
- States there are **no unpaid fees**
- Required by lenders for loan approval
- Allows smooth closing

#### "Dirty" 6(d) Certificate
- Shows unpaid amounts owed
- Must be resolved before closing (paid off) or negotiated
- Lender may refuse to close with unpaid fees

### Super-Lien Priority
- Condominium lien has **priority over first mortgage** for:
  - Up to **6 months** of unpaid condo fees
  - ALL attorney's fees and collection costs
- Makes condo liens very powerful collection tool

### Legal Effect
- Certificate "shall operate to discharge the unit from any lien for other sums then unpaid"
- Statement is **binding on the organization of unit owners**
- Accuracy is critical - trustees liable if incorrect

### Timeline Impact
- Request certificate **10-14 days before closing** (allows buffer)
- Association has 10 days to respond by law
- If "dirty," add time to negotiate payment/resolution
- Reminder triggers: P&S+7 days, Closing-14 days

### Fees
- Typically $100-$300 depending on association
- May include rush fees for expedited requests

### Citations
- **Statute**: M.G.L. Chapter 183A, Section 6(d)
- **Official Law**: https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartII/TitleI/Chapter183a/Section6
- **Legal Analysis**: https://www.mbmllc.com/6d-certificates-massachusetts-condo-law.html
- **Practice Guide**: https://www.pulgininorton.com/section-6-d-certificates.html

## 6. Municipal Lien Certificate

### What
Legal document listing all unpaid taxes, assessments, water rates, and utility charges on a property.

### When
- **Request Timing**: During attorney's title examination, typically **2-3 weeks before closing**
- **Issuance Timeframe**: Collector of taxes must provide within **10 days** of written request (excluding Saturdays, Sundays, holidays)
- **Validity**: Must be **filed for record within 150 days** of issuance date

### Who
- **Requesting Party**: Buyer's attorney (or seller's attorney)
- **Issuing Authority**: Municipal Tax Collector
- **Recording**: Registry of Deeds

### Requirements (M.G.L. c.60 §23)

Certificate must include:

1. **Property Taxes**: All unpaid real estate taxes
2. **Assessments**: Water/sewer betterments, sidewalk assessments, etc.
3. **Water Rates**: Unpaid water bills and charges
4. **Municipal Utility Charges**: Municipal lighting plant charges
5. **Other Municipal Charges**: Any other municipal liens

### Legal Effect
- When filed for record (within 150 days), certificate **discharges the property** from liens for all taxes, assessments, rates, and charges that do not appear on the certificate
- Provides clean title to buyer
- Protects buyer and lender from hidden municipal liens

### Fees
- Typically **$25-$50** per certificate (varies by municipality)
- Some municipalities charge more for rush service

### Special Considerations

#### Properties Requiring Final Water Reading
- **Boston, Lynn, Lawrence, Haverhill**: Final water reading required (except condos)
- **Boston Specific**: Takes approximately **2 weeks** to get final reading
- **Important**: Day-of-closing water bill is NOT sufficient (billing backlog)

### Timeline Impact
- Request certificate **3 weeks before closing** (allows 10-day issuance + buffer)
- If unpaid amounts found, add time to resolve (typically paid at closing via attorney escrow)
- For Boston/Lynn/Lawrence/Haverhill: Order **final water reading 3-4 weeks before closing**
- Reminder triggers: P&S+7 days, Closing-21 days

### Citations
- **Statute**: M.G.L. Chapter 60, Section 23
- **Official Law**: https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleIX/Chapter60/Section23
- **Practice Guide**: https://jrmccarthy-law.com/2020/12/29/ma-real-estate-law-municipal-lien-certificates/
- **Boston Water**: https://www.bwsc.org/residential-customers/services/buying-or-selling-property

## 7. Water Bill Final Reading

### What
Final meter reading and water bill calculation for properties with municipal water service.

### When
- **Request Timing**: **2-3 weeks before closing**
- **Processing Time**: Typically 1-2 weeks (Boston: approximately 2 weeks)

### Who
- **Requesting Party**: Seller or seller's attorney
- **Issuing Authority**: Municipal Water/Sewer Commission or Department
- **Payment Responsibility**: Seller (pro-rated to closing date)

### Applicability
**Required for**:
- Boston
- Lynn
- Lawrence
- Haverhill

**Exception**: Condominium units (water typically included in common expenses)

**Other Municipalities**: May require or may be included in Municipal Lien Certificate

### Process
1. Contact municipal water department to request final reading
2. Provide closing date and property information
3. Water department schedules meter reading
4. Final bill calculated pro-rated to closing date
5. Bill must be paid at or before closing
6. Receipt or paid bill provided to buyer's attorney

### Important Notes
- **Day-of-closing water bill is NOT sufficient** (especially Boston)
- Water departments often have billing backlogs
- Unpaid water bills can become municipal liens on property
- Water charges have lien priority similar to property taxes

### Timeline Impact
- Order final reading **3-4 weeks before closing** (especially Boston)
- Allow **2-3 weeks processing time**
- Payment typically handled via attorney escrow at closing
- Reminder triggers: P&S+7 days, Closing-28 days

### Citations
- **Boston Water and Sewer Commission**: https://www.bwsc.org/residential-customers/services/buying-or-selling-property
- **Practice Note**: https://www.alavibraza.com/importantinformationregardingyourcl

## 8. Buyer/Lender Milestones

### What
Key deadlines and milestones driven by buyer's financing and due diligence that seller/agent should track.

### Why Track These
- Seller needs to know if buyer is on track to close
- Early identification of buyer issues allows time to resolve or find backup buyer
- Proactive agent demonstrates professionalism and protects seller's interests

---

### 8.1 Loan Commitment

**What**: Lender's written commitment to provide mortgage financing to buyer.

**Timing**:
- **Typical**: 3-4 weeks after Purchase & Sale Agreement signed
- **P&S Contingency**: Usually 30-45 days from P&S for buyer to obtain commitment

**Key Points**:
- Commitment letter includes conditions that must be met
- Buyer must satisfy conditions before mortgage contingency date expires
- If buyer cannot get commitment, can request extension (must be in writing)
- If buyer fails to obtain commitment by deadline, may be able to terminate P&S and recover deposit

**Seller/Agent Action Items**:
- Know the mortgage contingency date from P&S
- Check in with buyer's agent 1 week before contingency date
- If extension requested, negotiate terms (how long, deposit at risk, etc.)

**Reminder Triggers**:
- P&S+21 days: Check loan progress
- Mortgage contingency date -7 days: Verify commitment received or extension needed

---

### 8.2 Property Appraisal

**What**: Professional valuation of property required by lender.

**Timing**:
- **Ordered**: 1-2 weeks after loan application (shortly after P&S)
- **Completed**: 1-2 weeks after ordered
- **Total**: Usually complete 2-3 weeks after P&S

**Cost**: $450-$1,000+ (paid by buyer)

**Key Points**:
- Appraisal must support (meet or exceed) sales price
- If appraisal comes in low, may require:
  - Buyer to bring additional cash
  - Seller to reduce price
  - Renegotiation or contract termination
- Ordered through Appraisal Management Company (AMC) - no direct contact with appraiser

**Seller/Agent Action Items**:
- Ensure property shows well for appraiser
- Provide agent with comparable sales to share
- If low appraisal, negotiate resolution quickly

**Reminder Triggers**:
- P&S+7 days: Confirm appraisal ordered
- P&S+21 days: Check if appraisal completed and value supports sales price

---

### 8.3 Home Inspection

**What**: Professional inspection of property condition (usually for buyer's due diligence).

**Timing**:
- **Typical**: Within 7-10 days of offer acceptance or P&S
- **Inspection Contingency**: Usually 7-15 days from P&S

**Key Points**:
- Buyer may request repairs or credits based on findings
- Major issues could lead to re-negotiation or buyer withdrawal
- Seller should be prepared for inspection results and have response strategy

**Seller/Agent Action Items**:
- Coordinate access for inspector
- Be prepared to respond to inspection report
- Negotiate repairs vs. credits vs. price reduction

**Reminder Triggers**:
- P&S+2 days: Expect inspection to be scheduled
- Inspection contingency date -3 days: Check status

---

### 8.4 Homeowners Insurance

**What**: Required insurance coverage for property (lender requirement).

**Timing**:
- **Application**: 2-3 weeks before closing
- **Delivery**: Policy or binder must be provided at closing

**Key Points**:
- Lender requires proof of insurance before releasing funds
- Policy must show lender as mortgagee/loss payee
- Coverage must meet or exceed loan amount
- Some properties difficult to insure (old roof, outdated systems, high-risk areas)

**Seller/Agent Action Items**:
- Less direct seller involvement, but good to confirm buyer has obtained
- Insurance issues can delay closing

**Reminder Triggers**:
- Closing -21 days: Confirm buyer has started insurance application
- Closing -7 days: Confirm policy or binder obtained

---

### 8.5 Final Walkthrough

**What**: Buyer's final inspection of property before closing.

**Timing**:
- **Typical**: 24-48 hours before closing
- **Purpose**: Verify property condition unchanged, agreed repairs completed, property vacant/broom-clean

**Key Points**:
- Buyer confirms property is as expected per contract
- All seller's belongings removed (unless negotiated to stay)
- Any negotiated repairs completed
- Utilities still on for inspection

**Seller/Agent Action Items**:
- Ensure property is clean and in agreed-upon condition
- Complete all negotiated repairs before walkthrough
- Be available to address any last-minute issues

**Reminder Triggers**:
- Closing -3 days: Schedule final walkthrough
- Closing -1 day: Confirm walkthrough completed successfully

---

### 8.6 Clear to Close

**What**: Final lender approval to proceed with closing.

**Timing**:
- **Typical**: 2-5 days before closing date
- **Requirement**: All lender conditions satisfied, final underwriting complete

**Key Points**:
- Lender has completed final review of buyer's finances
- Final Closing Disclosure delivered to buyer (3 business days before closing)
- Funding confirmed
- Wire transfer instructions finalized

**Seller/Agent Action Items**:
- Confirm with buyer's agent that "clear to close" received
- If not received 3 days before closing, investigate potential delays

**Reminder Triggers**:
- Closing -5 days: Check for clear to close status
- Closing -2 days: Confirm closing is still on schedule

## 9. Task Matrix Summary

- **M** = Mandatory
- **C** = Common/Recommended

## Template Design Implications

Based on this research, the Template DSL should support:

### 1. Conditional Logic
```yaml
applies_if: |
  property.year_built < 1978 AND
  property.type IN ['SFH', 'Condo', 'Multifamily']
```

### 2. Relative Due Dates
```yaml
due: "P&S+7d"          # 7 days after Purchase & Sale
due: "Closing-21d"     # 21 days before closing
due: "Offer+2d"        # 2 days after offer acceptance
```

### 3. Property Type Branching
```yaml
# Title 5 task only appears if:
applies_if: |
  property.has_septic == true AND
  property.type != 'Condo'
```

### 4. Municipality-Specific Tasks
```yaml
# Final water reading only for certain cities
applies_if: |
  property.city IN ['Boston', 'Lynn', 'Lawrence', 'Haverhill'] AND
  property.type != 'Condo'
```

### 5. Multi-Stage Reminders
```yaml
reminders:
  - offset: "-21d"  # 3 weeks before
    channels: ["email"]
  - offset: "-7d"   # 1 week before
    channels: ["email", "sms"]
  - offset: "-1d"   # Day before
    channels: ["email", "sms"]
```

### 6. Dependency Tracking
```yaml
dependencies:
  - "ps_agreement_signed"
  - "property_vacant"
```

### 7. External Party Tracking
```yaml
assignee: "FireDept"
vendor_type: "smoke_co_inspection"
contact_method: "phone_or_portal"
estimated_response_time: "7-10d"
```
